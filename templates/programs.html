AI Pedagogy Labs

Monthly interactive sessions where faculty experiment with AI tools and explore pedagogical strategies in a supportive, judgment-free environment. Each lab focuses on a specific theme relevant to teaching and learning.

Lab 3: AI Policies

The third AI Pedagogy Lab focused on faculty AI policies. Fourteen instructors reviewed survey data from 44 WPI faculty, examined anonymized peer policies from their own discipline cluster, and discussed in small groups before sharing with the room. Several themes emerged, such as adopting an AI policy is the instructor's responsibility; it should not be outsourced to students. The group drew a useful distinction between policy and guidelines: a policy is enforced, a guideline is encouraged. One participant questioned whether calling something a policy is meaningful without a realistic enforcement mechanism. The room agreed that students engage more seriously when they can see a direct connection between the policy and course objectives, and customizing the policy assignment by assignment produces better outcomes than a single blanket statement. Lab 2: Assignment and Assessment Redesign

The second AI Pedagogy focused on redesigning assignments in the age of AI. Eight instructors participated in a discussion that began with an overview of the current state of AI in assignment answers. They shared their own experiences and insights. The discussion topics and references are attached for your reference. Next, the attendees were grouped based on the assignment formats they encounter in their courses. These formats include coding and computational tasks, labs and experiment-based tasks, problem-solving and analysis tasks, writing and reflection tasks, design and project-based tasks. For each group, the instructors developed learning outcomes that align with AI while ensuring that they meet the course objectives. Finally, each individual received an assignment redesign worksheet. This sheet contains specific instructions for redesigning one of their course assignments. The redesign sheet is also attached for your reference.

Food for Thought

Quarterly, informal discussion series that brings faculty and students together to explore how AI is shaping learning, teaching, and preparation for the future. Each session focuses on a timely theme in AI pedagogy and creates space for open conversation across roles and disciplines.

FFT 1: Navigating AI Together

This session brought faculty and students together to discuss baseline survey results comparing their perspectives on AI use in academic settings. Key findings revealed that while adoption rates are similar across groups, one-third of students reported being against AI use compared to only 12% of faculty. The discussion explored tensions around AI policies, academic integrity, and the impact of AI on foundational learning.
